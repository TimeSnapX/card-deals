      Spotting undervalued listings

      General guidance, not financial advice. Always check sold prices for the exact card before you buy.

      Where the bargains hide

      • Misspelt titles. “Charzard”, “Lebron Jame”, “Pokeman”. Fewer people find them, so fewer bids.
      • Bad photos. Dark, blurry or sleeved-in-a-toploader shots put buyers off. Ask for clear front/back photos before buying.
      • Lots and bundles. Sellers often price by the stack. One key card can be worth more than the whole lot.
      • Odd ending times. Auctions ending at 3–5am AEST, midweek, or on public holidays get fewer last-minute bids.
      • Vague titles. No set, number or year (“old pokemon card holo”) means the seller may not know what they have.
      • Wrong category. Cards listed under toys, collectables or the wrong sport get less traffic.
      • Best Offer. Fixed-price listings with Best Offer often take 10–20% less. Offers are only a starting point, and buying is up to you.

      Rare gems

      • Low population graded cards. Check the PSA/BGS/CGC pop report: a PSA 10 with a tiny population sells for a big premium.
      • Errors and misprints. Some (wrong backs, missing stamps, famous print errors) are valuable; most miscuts aren't. Price the exact error.
      • Short prints and numbered cards. /99, /25, /10 and 1/1 parallels: the lower the print run, the higher the price. Check it's the real parallel.
      • 1st Edition, shadowless and promos. Small marks matter a lot in vintage Pokémon. Compare the symbol and border to a reference image.

      Red flags

      • Price far below every sold comp with stock photos: could be fake, a reprint, or a scam.
      • Words like “RP”, “reprint”, “proxy”, “custom”, “orica”, “replica” mean it isn't an original.
      • New seller, no feedback, pushing to pay outside the platform: walk away.
      • Slab looks tampered with or the cert number doesn't match the card: check the cert on the grader's website.

      How the deal score works

      • Discount (0–60): −20% or worse scores 0, 50% under market or better scores 60, linear in between.
      • Data depth (0–25): comps: 3–5 = 10, 6–9 = 18, 10+ = 25. Live prices: sales-based and confirmed by a second source = 18, one sales-based source = 12, asking prices only or sources disagree = 6.
      • Consistency (0–15): comps spread under 15% = 15, under 30% = 9, wider = 3. Live prices = 8.
      • Verdict: 30%+ under = Strong deal, 15–30% = Good deal, 5–15% = Slightly under, ±5% = About market, 5–20% over = Above market, more = Overpriced. Your cost includes shipping.
